--- title: "What to Expect on Your First Camping Trip" description: "Honest advice about bathrooms, bugs, bears, weather, and other realities of first-time camping near the Appalachian Trail." --- # What to Expect

Camping for the first time can be intimidating. Here's the honest truth about what it's really like — no sugarcoating.

Bathrooms & Showers

  • Tohickon Valley: Flush toilets + hot showers in the comfort station. Some of the best amenities you'll get at a campground.
  • Delaware Water Gap primitive sites: No toilets or showers. Dig a cat hole (6+ inches deep, 300 ft from water) and pack out toilet paper.
  • Dingmans Campground / KOA: Full bathhouses with flush toilets and showers.

If you're nervous about toilet situations, start with Tohickon Valley — it's basically "camping with training wheels."

Bugs

Pennsylvania has an abundance of insects, especially near water. Here's what to expect:

  • Mosquitoes — worst at dusk and dawn. Bring DEET or picaridin repellent. A thermocell works great at camp.
  • Ticks — Lyme disease is prevalent in PA. Check yourself thoroughly after hiking. Tick key ($5) is a great tool.
  • Flies / gnats — annoying but harmless. A head net is cheap insurance.

Bears & Wildlife

Black bears are present in every area mentioned on this site. However, they are generally shy and avoid humans. Key rules:

  • Never store food in your tent. Use the provided bear box (if available), hang a bear bag, or use a bear canister.
  • Coolers should be locked in your car at night, NOT left out.
  • Make noise while hiking to avoid surprising a bear.
  • You'll likely see deer, raccoons, squirrels, and birds. Raccoons will absolutely try to steal your food — don't leave anything out overnight.

Weather Realities

  • Summer afternoons often bring brief thunderstorms — have a rain fly and tarp ready
  • Nighttime temps drop 20-30°F from daytime highs — bring warm layers
  • Humidity is high in PA summer — you'll feel sticky
  • Check DWG weather page before you go

Cell Service

  • Tohickon Valley: Generally has some cell service (varies by carrier)
  • Delaware Water Gap: Very spotty to none. Download maps and directions ahead of time.
  • Dingmans Campground: Proudly advertises as a cell dead zone — "truly unplug"

Sleeping Comfort

A few things that surprise new campers:

  • It gets dark — true dark, unlike your bedroom. A headlamp is essential.
  • It gets noisy — owls, frogs, rustling leaves, distant coyotes. Earplugs help if you're a light sleeper.
  • Or it gets silent — so silent you hear your own heartbeat. Some people find this unsettling.
  • The ground is harder than you expect. A good sleeping pad is worth the investment.

Etiquette

  • Quiet hours (typically 10 PM – 7 AM) — respect your neighbors
  • Keep your site clean — don't leave food scraps or trash
  • Don't cut live trees for firewood — use downed wood or buy bundles
  • Leave No Trace: pack out everything you brought in
  • Be friendly! Campers are a welcoming community. Ask neighbors for help or a lighter if needed.

If Things Go Wrong

  • Can't start a fire? — ask a neighbor or the camp host
  • Forgot something? — Tohickon Valley is 10 min from a Wawa in Pipersville
  • Too cold/wet/uncomfortable? — you can always pack up and go home. No shame in it.
  • Medical emergency? — call 911. Doylestown Hospital is ~20 min from Tohickon Valley.
